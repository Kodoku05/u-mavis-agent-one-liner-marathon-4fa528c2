# Original candidates

Written in this session. Scores are out of five; they are editorial judgments, not external judge results.

1. Corporate culture: The CEO told us to bring our whole selves to work, so I invited his second family to the Christmas party. Attendance corrected.
2. Housing policy: The mayor solved homelessness by calling park benches studio apartments. I listed his mansion as a shelter. Zoning dispute resolved.
3. Surgery: My surgeon said he could operate in his sleep, so I reported him to anesthesia. Privileges suspended pending a nap.

Each uses a high-status target, a perverse response with consequences, and an administrative closing line. The CEO faces exposure at a work event; the mayor faces conversion of his own property; the surgeon faces suspension. The housing line requires more setup and the surgery line stretches the role of anesthesia as a reporting authority. The corporate line makes the policy itself cause the executive's problem, with the attendance note treating the revelation as routine office administration.

## Selection and single refinement

Winner: corporate culture. Replace “told us to” with “said to” to cut a word without losing the premise. Keep “Christmas” because the specific occasion makes the family collision easy to picture.

Final: The CEO said to bring our whole selves to work, so I invited his second family to the Christmas party. Attendance corrected.
